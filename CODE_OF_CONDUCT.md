# Code of Conduct

This file sets the rules of behavior for the llm-gauntlet community. It applies to everyone who takes part in the project.

## Our promise

We want the project to be a safe and friendly place for everyone. This includes all ages, experience levels, backgrounds, and identities. Many of our users and contributors are students. We want them to feel welcome and to learn.

We promise to treat each other with respect. We promise to give and accept feedback with kindness.

## Where this applies

This code applies in all project spaces:

- The repository, including issues, pull requests, discussions, and code review comments.
- Any chat, call, or meeting that the project runs.
- Public spaces, when a person speaks for the project or acts as part of the project.

## Expected behavior

- Be kind and patient. Answer questions in plain words. No question is too small.
- Respect other people and their time.
- Welcome new contributors. Help them to find their first task.
- Give feedback on the work, not on the person.
- Accept feedback with grace. Say thank you for a review.
- Assume good intent. Ask a question before you assume a bad one.
- Admit a mistake. Fix it and learn from it.
- Credit the work of other people.
- Use the language that fits a mixed group. Write in short, clear sentences. Explain terms that a beginner may not know.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Insults, put-downs, or personal attacks.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Discrimination or hate speech about a person's age, body, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, experience level, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.
- Sexual language or images, and unwanted sexual attention.
- Threats or violent language.
- Trolling and repeated disruptive comments.
- Sharing the private information of another person, such as an address or an email address, without consent. This includes doxxing.
- Sharing a secret that belongs to another person, such as a session file, a token, or a key.
- Pressure on a maintainer or a contributor to merge or to answer fast.
- Spam, and unrelated promotion.
- Any other behavior that a reasonable person would call unprofessional in a project space.

## Rules for project content

These rules come from the project, and they apply with this code:

- Do not post or request code that bypasses a CAPTCHA, a rate limit, or a usage limit.
- Do not post or request code that hides the automation from a provider.
- Do not post a real session file, a real key, a real cookie, or a real token. Use fake values.
- Do not ask other people to share an account or a password.
- Do not use the project to harm another person or an account that is not yours.

A comment or a pull request that breaks these rules can be removed.

## Reporting a problem

If you see or feel unacceptable behavior, report it. You do not need proof. You may report a problem that you saw happen to another person.

1. Open the page `https://github.com/JatinSri1909/llm-gauntlet/security/advisories/new`. This page is private. Only the maintainers can read it.
2. Write "Code of Conduct" in the title.
3. Say what happened, where, when, and who was there. Add links if you have them.
4. Say if you want us to contact you, and how.

If you cannot use this page, open a public issue that says only "I need to report a Code of Conduct problem." Do not write any detail in that issue. A maintainer will give you a private way to send the report.

The maintainers will:

- Read each report and keep it private.
- Confirm that they got it, within 7 days.
- Look at the facts and listen to the people who are involved.
- Decide what to do and tell the reporter what they decided, when it is safe to do so.
- Protect the reporter. A maintainer will not punish or expose a person for a report that they made in good faith.

A maintainer who is part of a report does not take part in the decision about it.

## What happens next

The maintainers decide how to act. They choose a response that fits the behavior. They use these steps as a guide.

| Step | Behavior | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Correction | A small breach, such as an unfriendly comment or an unclear joke | A private note that explains the problem. An apology may be asked. |
| 2. Warning | A larger breach, or repeated behavior after a correction | A written warning. The person must stop the behavior. The person may lose access to the project spaces for a set time. |
| 3. Temporary ban | A serious breach, or no change after a warning | No contact and no access to the project spaces for a set time. |
| 4. Permanent ban | A pattern of breaches, harassment, or an act of violence or threat | A permanent end of access to the project spaces. |

The maintainers may skip steps for a serious breach. A maintainer can edit, hide, or delete a comment, a commit, an issue, or a pull request that breaks this code. A maintainer can lock a thread.

A person who disagrees with a decision can reply to the maintainers in the same private report. A maintainer who was not part of the first decision will look at the reply when one is available.

## Our duties as maintainers

The maintainers must follow this code too. The maintainers are responsible for making the rules clear and for applying them in a fair way. They do not apply them based on who the person is.

## Scope of the maintainers' power

The maintainers act only in project spaces. They do not judge behavior in private life that has no link to the project, except when it puts a member of the community at risk.

## Changes to this code

A maintainer can change this file. A change to this file needs a pull request and a review, like any other change. The community can ask for a change through an issue.

## Questions

Open an issue or a discussion with the question label. Ask in plain words.
